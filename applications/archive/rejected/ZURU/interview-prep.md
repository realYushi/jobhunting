# ZURU First Interview Prep

Role: **Junior AI Integration Engineer**  
Format: likely **intro + motivation + behavioral / practical AI questions**

## 1) What they are probably screening for

In a first Teams interview, they are likely checking:

- can you explain your background clearly
- do you genuinely want **this** role, not just any AI job
- can you work with non-technical teams
- do you have real, hands-on AI builder experience
- are you honest about gaps and still able to learn fast

One useful signal: the application page asked about:

- a real project where you used AI tools
- a recent AI tool/technique you explored
- how you would choose what to automate first in a team

So expect some version of those questions.

## 2) Your strongest angle

Your best positioning:

> I’m a full-stack product engineer who likes turning messy, real workflows into useful tools. My background combines TypeScript/Python engineering with recent hands-on AI work: agent workflows at GrowLab, a coding-agent platform at Decoded, and a RAG chatbot project for Truss House. What makes ZURU especially attractive is that the role is practical and cross-functional: sit with teams, find friction, build a working PoC, and help it move toward production.

That is the core story to keep coming back to.

## How would you decide what to automate first in a team?

This is especially likely because it appeared on the application page.

Use this structure:

1. observe the workflow
2. find repetitive, high-friction steps
3. choose a narrow use case with clear inputs/outputs
4. build a small PoC quickly
5. test with the team
6. measure whether it saves time or reduces manual work
7. only then expand or productionize

Sample answer:

> I’d start by shadowing the team and understanding where time is going. I’d look for tasks that are repetitive, frequent, and painful, with inputs and outputs that are clear enough to prototype around. Then I’d pick a narrow use case first, build a quick proof of concept, and test it with the people doing the work. I’d want to learn whether it actually saves effort and fits their workflow before trying to scale it. My bias would be toward solving one real problem cleanly rather than automating everything at once.

## 7) Story bank you can reuse

Prepare these 4 stories in STAR format.

### Story 1: Decoded - coding-agent platform

- **Situation:** internship, building a full-stack coding-agent platform
- **Task:** help build the core agent and keep outputs aligned with specs
- **Action:** worked on the agent flow and AI-assisted review workflow with human review
- **Result:** strong example of practical agent tooling and structured AI use

### Story 2: GrowLab - AI in product workflows

- **Situation:** current full-stack product engineering role
- **Task:** build internal tools and AI-powered workflows that improve usability or speed
- **Action:** integrate AI where it helps and turn ideas into prototypes, then stronger production features
- **Result:** shows practical judgment, not blind AI enthusiasm

### Story 3: Truss House - RAG chatbot

- **Situation:** construction-tech client needed a customer-facing information tool
- **Task:** build a chatbot over product documentation
- **Action:** used Next.js, semantic search, and embeddings; kept the widget lazy-loaded to avoid hurting initial load performance
- **Result:** strong evidence for RAG, retrieval thinking, and user-focused implementation

### Story 4: HALO - real-time dashboard

- **Situation:** farm effluent control monitoring dashboard
- **Task:** make operational data easier to understand in real time
- **Action:** built frontend with Vue/TypeScript and added map-based GeoJSON visualization
- **Result:** shows product delivery, learning speed, and clear real-world use case

## 8) Things to avoid saying

- don’t claim AWS / Terraform / Databricks experience you do not have
- don’t sound like you only want “an AI job” in general
- don’t overuse buzzwords like “revolutionary” or “disruptive” yourself
- don’t make everything about the model; keep bringing it back to workflows and users

## 9) Good questions to ask them

Pick 3.

1. What does a strong first 90 days look like in this role?
2. What kinds of teams would this role work with first?
3. How do you decide whether an AI prototype is ready to hand off for production?
4. What tools and infrastructure does the team use most today?
5. What makes someone especially successful at ZURU in a cross-functional role like this?
6. For the one-week sprints mentioned in the role, what usually makes a sprint successful?

## 10) Teams interview checklist

- have the job description open
- have your CV and this document open
- prepare 3 stories you can tell clearly in 1-2 minutes each
- test camera, mic, lighting, and screen name
- keep answers clear and slightly shorter than you think
- pause before answering; do not rush

## 11) Best final message to leave them with

If they ask anything like “anything else you’d like us to know?”

> I’m excited by roles where AI is used to solve real operational problems, not just as a demo. That’s what makes this ZURU role stand out to me. I think my full-stack background, recent applied AI experience, and interest in working closely with real teams make this a strong fit, and I’d be excited to contribute and learn quickly.
